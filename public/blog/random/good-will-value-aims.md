# Good will, value, aims

The terms under which I take any commercial work are also the ideology
under which I produce my best work. My tools have to be made available
gratis or for free, with access not artificially restricted by opaque
bolt-ons. Whatever represents my identity on the internet has to be
something I can visualise, debug and maintain by hand, with no
restriction on who can reach it. A platform that cannot offer that is a
business-cessation scenario for me, and I decline the service.

**Good will** — I bear the company named in the record below no bad
will. The reference stays on my public site entirely as an extension of
what I claim here: the terms are about how software represents a
person, not against anyone who has yet to honour them. The messages are
kept verbatim because a paraphrase would soften what was actually said.

**Value** — for the past nine years, the work has been keeping free and
opensource software public: spreading awareness of its existence,
educating on its use, and offering tuition and help with operating
countless public projects. As a freelance individual, not a trusted
and/or industry-backed business, that public record is what extends my
credibility — which is why public access is a principle, not a
preference.

**Aims** — public access, with no restriction from any person with
access to the internet. What represents my identity online is software
I can visualise, debug and maintain by hand. A web platform that limits
the session to itself — zero configuration, zero forwarding, zero
programmatic access, no ownership over what is written through it —
cannot carry that.
